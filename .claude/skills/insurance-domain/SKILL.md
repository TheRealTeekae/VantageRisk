# Insurance Domain Knowledge — Vantage Risk

This skill encodes practitioner-level P&C insurance knowledge at the level of an
experienced risk manager. Do not treat insurance concepts generically. Apply this
knowledge when extracting data from documents, building analysis logic, and generating
renewal intelligence reports.

---

## The Renewal Cycle

The renewal cycle is the core workflow Vantage Risk serves. Understanding it determines
what matters when and why.

**Typical timeline (working backward from expiration):**

| Timeframe Before Expiration | Activity |
|---|---|
| 180+ days | Risk manager reviews program, identifies gaps, prepares renewal strategy |
| 120 days | Broker begins marketing; submissions go to carriers |
| 90 days | Underwriters review loss runs, submissions, exposure data |
| 60 days | Carrier indications received; negotiation begins |
| 30 days | Bind orders issued; policy terms finalized |
| Expiration | Coverage effective |

**What underwriters are evaluating:**
- Loss history (frequency, severity, trend)
- Exposure growth or contraction (payroll, revenue, property values, fleet size)
- Risk quality narrative — what has the insured done to control losses?
- Program structure — are limits adequate? Are retentions appropriate for the risk?

Vantage Risk output should help the risk manager answer all four of these before the
broker submits the account.

## Loss Runs

Loss runs are the most important document in the renewal process. They are the primary
source of truth for underwriters evaluating an account.

### What a Loss Run Contains

A loss run is a report generated by the current carrier showing all claims on a policy
for a given policy period. Key fields:

| Field | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Claim number** | Unique identifier for each claim |
| **Date of loss** | When the incident occurred |
| **Date reported** | When the claim was reported to the carrier |
| **Claimant** | Who was injured or what was damaged |
| **Description** | Brief narrative of what happened |
| **Paid losses** | Amount already paid out to date |
| **Reserves** | Carrier's estimate of future payments |
| **Total incurred** | Paid + reserves = total expected cost |
| **Status** | Open (still active) or Closed |

### How to Read Loss Development

Loss runs are typically requested for **5 years** of history, with each year evaluated
at multiple points in time (called "development periods"). This shows whether losses
are growing or shrinking over time.

- **Favorable development:** Reserves decreasing over time = losses settling for less
  than expected. Good signal for underwriters.
- **Adverse development:** Reserves increasing over time = losses worse than expected.
  Red flag that requires explanation.

### Key Metrics to Extract and Calculate

- **Claim count by year** — frequency trend
- **Total incurred by year** — severity trend
- **Large loss count** — claims above a threshold (typically $25K, $50K, or $100K
  depending on the program size)
- **Open claim count** — how many claims are still active
- **Loss ratio** — total incurred / premium paid (if premium data is available)
- **Frequency rate** — claims per unit of exposure (per $1M revenue, per employee, etc.)

### Red Flags to Flag in the Report

- Frequency increasing year-over-year for 2+ consecutive years
- Any single claim exceeding 25% of the annual aggregate limit
- High open claim count on older policy years (suggests slow claim resolution)
- Adverse reserve development on claims more than 3 years old
- Clustering of similar claim types (slip/fall concentration, auto liability pattern, etc.)

## Lines of Business — Key Differences

Each line renews differently and has different underwriting triggers. Vantage Risk must
handle them distinctly.

### Commercial Property

- **What it covers:** Physical assets — buildings, contents, business income
- **Key data points:** Total insured values (TIV), location schedule, construction type,
  occupancy, protection class, catastrophe exposure (wind, flood, earthquake zones)
- **Renewal drivers:** TIV changes, recent CAT events, reinsurance market conditions
- **Common gaps:** Coinsurance clauses, business income period too short, flood/earthquake
  sublimits below actual exposure, agreed value vs. replacement cost distinction
- **Underwriting red flags:** Vacant locations, deferred maintenance, prior losses
  from same peril

### General Liability (GL)

- **What it covers:** Third-party bodily injury and property damage claims
- **Key data points:** Revenue, payroll, operations description, prior loss history
- **Renewal drivers:** Revenue growth, change in operations, litigation environment
- **Common gaps:** Contractual liability exclusions, completed operations limits,
  product liability sublimits for manufacturers
- **Underwriting red flags:** High frequency of slip/fall claims, prior large verdicts,
  operations involving higher-risk activities (liquor liability, habitational, etc.)

### Umbrella / Excess Liability

- **What it covers:** Liability limits above the underlying GL, auto, and employers
  liability policies
- **Key data points:** Underlying policy limits and carriers, attachment point,
  total limit purchased
- **Renewal drivers:** Increased jury verdicts (social inflation), changes to
  underlying program
- **Common gaps:** Gaps in underlying policy coverage that break the umbrella tower,
  manuscript exclusions that differ from underlying policy, drop-down provisions
- **Underwriting red flags:** Any underlying policy with a large open claim near
  its limit

### Cyber Liability

- **What it covers:** Data breaches, ransomware, business interruption from cyber events,
  third-party liability for data exposure
- **Key data points:** Revenue, employee count, data types held (PII, PHI, PCI),
  security controls, MFA status, EDR/backup systems
- **Renewal drivers:** Security posture changes, ransomware market conditions,
  prior incidents
- **Common gaps:** Sublimits on ransomware, social engineering fraud, system failure
  vs. security failure distinction
- **Underwriting red flags:** No MFA on email/remote access, no offline backups,
  prior ransomware incident

### Workers' Compensation

- **What it covers:** Employee injury — medical and lost wages — statutory in each state
- **Key data points:** Payroll by state and class code, experience modification factor
  (e-mod), prior loss history
- **Renewal drivers:** Payroll changes, e-mod changes, state rate filings
- **Common gaps:** Misclassified employees (independent contractors), incorrect class
  codes, employer liability limits too low
- **Underwriting red flags:** E-mod above 1.20, high frequency of soft tissue claims,
  prior large lost-time claims still open

### Commercial Auto

- **What it covers:** Liability and physical damage for owned, hired, and non-owned vehicles
- **Key data points:** Vehicle schedule, driver list and MVRs, annual mileage, radius
  of operations
- **Renewal drivers:** Fleet changes, driver record changes, prior loss history
- **Common gaps:** Non-owned auto (employees using personal vehicles for work),
  hired auto limits, gap between auto and umbrella
- **Underwriting red flags:** Multiple at-fault accidents in prior 3 years,
  DUI/serious violations on driver list, high-value fleet with poor physical damage history

## Coverage Gap Detection Logic

When analyzing a program, flag the following patterns:

**Limit adequacy:**
- Primary GL limit below $1M per occurrence is unusual for any commercial account
- Umbrella limit should be evaluated against the account's largest single exposure
- Property limits should reflect current replacement cost, not historical purchase price
  (inflation gap is a common problem post-2020)

**Sublimit traps:**
- Flood and earthquake sublimits that are a fraction of TIV for exposed locations
- Business income sublimit with a period of indemnity shorter than actual rebuild time
- Cyber sublimits on ransomware that are below the account's realistic ransomware exposure
- Inland marine/equipment sublimits that don't cover newly acquired property automatically

**Exclusion flags:**
- Contractual liability exclusions on GL that would void coverage for indemnification
  agreements in vendor contracts
- Pollution exclusions on GL for accounts with environmental exposure
- Professional liability exclusions on GL for accounts providing advice or services
- EIFS/exterior insulation exclusions on property for applicable buildings

**Structural issues:**
- Retained limits (SIR or deductible) that are too high relative to cash flow
- No continuity of coverage between expiring and renewing policy (gap in coverage period)
- Underlying limits that don't meet umbrella attachment requirements

## Carrier Narrative Framing

The renewal narrative draft should position the account favorably to underwriters.
Underwriters read hundreds of submissions. A well-framed narrative stands out.

**Structure of a good renewal narrative:**

1. **Account overview** — who the insured is, what they do, scale of operations
2. **Risk quality statement** — what makes this a well-managed account
3. **Loss history context** — acknowledge adverse years, explain what changed
4. **Exposure changes** — growth, contraction, or structural changes since last renewal
5. **Forward-looking statement** — why the account is a good long-term risk

**Language principles:**
- Be specific, not generic ("implemented quarterly safety audits with recorded
  completion rates" not "improved safety program")
- Address bad loss years directly — silence reads as hiding something
- Frame frequency as managed ("claims resolved quickly at low average severity")
  when frequency is the issue
- Frame severity as isolated ("one large loss in five years, not a frequency problem")
  when severity is the issue

## Data Extraction Priorities by Document Type

### From a Policy Document
Extract in this order:
1. Named insured and policy period
2. Line of business and carrier
3. Limits of liability (per occurrence and aggregate)
4. Deductible or SIR
5. Key sublimits
6. Key exclusions (especially any manuscript endorsements)
7. Premium

### From a Loss Run
Extract in this order:
1. Policy period covered
2. Line of business
3. Total claim count
4. Total incurred (paid + reserves)
5. Open vs. closed claim breakdown
6. Large losses (flag anything above $25K)
7. Claim descriptions for any large or unusual losses

### From Market Data / Benchmarks
Extract in this order:
1. Rate change indication for the relevant line (hardening vs. softening)
2. Capacity notes (are carriers pulling back or expanding?)
3. Limit benchmarks for the account's industry and size
4. Any specific underwriting appetite changes

## Terminology Reference

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **TIV** | Total Insured Value — total property values on a policy |
| **E-mod** | Experience modification factor — WC rating based on loss history vs. industry average. 1.0 = average. Above 1.0 = worse than average. |
| **SIR** | Self-Insured Retention — similar to a deductible but insured handles claims within the retention |
| **Occurrence** | A single event giving rise to a claim |
| **Aggregate** | Maximum the policy pays in a policy period across all occurrences |
| **Sublimit** | A lower limit that applies to a specific coverage within a broader policy |
| **Manuscript endorsement** | A custom, non-standard policy modification — always flag these |
| **Loss development** | How reserves on a claim change over time |
| **Hard market** | Carriers restricting capacity, raising rates, tightening terms |
| **Soft market** | Carriers competing aggressively, lowering rates, broadening terms |
| **Social inflation** | Trend of increasing jury verdicts beyond economic damages |
| **Admitted carrier** | Licensed and regulated by the state — claims protected by state guaranty fund |
| **Non-admitted (E&S)** | Not state-licensed — more flexibility on terms but no guaranty fund protection |
| **Binder** | Temporary evidence of coverage while final policy is being issued |
| **Certificate of Insurance** | Evidence of coverage issued to a third party — not the actual policy |
